02 · HEAD TO HEAD

Career & season trends

Regular-season career totals, then how each passer's yardage and touchdowns tracked year over year.

The "1st-Team AP" column, worth sitting with

That's Associated Press First-Team All-Pro, the annual vote by roughly 50 national media members for the single best player at each position that season. It's a different honor from MVP, and a stricter one: Brady was voted the outright best quarterback in the league in only 3 of his 23 seasons (2007, 2010, 2017). Manning did it 7 times, more than double Brady's total, and Rodgers did it 4. Even by the voters who covered him every week, Brady wasn't seen as the best quarterback in football in the other 20 seasons of his career, the rings and counting stats piled up regardless.

Postseason splits

Brady's counting stats dominate the playoff and Super Bowl record books, largely because he's played in nearly twice as many postseason games as anyone else here. Rate stats tell a closer story.

Regular season vs. playoffs

A closer look at efficiency, not just volume: passer rating consistently tells a different story than yards per game once the games start mattering most.

Reading the two columns

Rating measures quality per throw, and it drops for Brady in the playoffs (97.2 to 89.8, down 7.6%), same direction as Brees and Manning, though not as steep. Yards per game measures total volume, and it actually rises for Brady in the postseason (267.9 to 279.2, up 4.2%), which makes sense: playoff games are closer and don't get out of hand the way some regular-season blowouts do, so there's more incentive to keep throwing deep into the fourth quarter. The one QB here who bucks the efficiency trend entirely is Mahomes, whose rating actually improves in the playoffs (up 6.5%), the only one of the five who does.

03 · THE CENTRAL ARGUMENT

The luck factor

The source deck's core thesis: Brady's teams got more help than any dynasty in modern NFL history, elite defenses, top-10 field position for two decades, and a remarkable number of opponent mistakes at the worst possible time. None of this is a knock on Brady's own play, it's an argument about how much of the credit belongs elsewhere.

The supporting cast

None of this is possible without an organization that kept stacking elite talent and coaching around him for two decades. A sample of who was lining up alongside him.

🔥 A quick rant about the Tampa Bay landing spot

Let's be honest about how soft this landing was. Brady didn't walk into a rebuild in 2020, he walked into a defense that was already elite. Todd Bowles took over as defensive coordinator in 2019, a full year before Brady ever put on a Bucs uniform, and that unit immediately became the NFL's best run defense that season, then repeated as the league's best run defense again in 2020. The turnaround everyone credits to Brady's arrival was already underway.

Then the seeding gods did him a favor too. Tampa Bay went 11-5 in 2020, one of the best records in the conference, and still had to open the playoffs on the road as a Wild Card team because Tampa Bay lost the NFC South to the Saints. Their reward: a trip to Washington, a 7-9 division winner that was the weakest team left in the field, playing in a stadium with zero fans in the stands due to COVID restrictions, silencing what should have been a hostile road environment. A first-round road game against a losing team with no crowd noise is about as close to a bye week as the playoffs get.

The AFC East advantage

Brady's Patriots didn't just win their division, they turned it into a formality. Continuity at quarterback, while his AFC East rivals cycled through journeymen and busts year after year, may be the single most underrated ingredient in the whole dynasty.

Automatic: the kicking game

For nearly his entire Patriots tenure, Brady never had to think about the kicking game. New England handed the job to Adam Vinatieri, then Stephen Gostkowski, and simply never had a bad year at the position, a luxury almost no other quarterback of his era enjoyed.

81.9% → 87.4%Vinatieri's (2000–2005), then Gostkowski's (2006–2019), regular-season field goal accuracy, two Hall-of-Fame-caliber kickers covering all 20 of Brady's Patriots seasons back to back
90% / 91.7%Vinatieri's and Gostkowski's conversion rate specifically on "clutch" kicks (tying or go-ahead, final 3 minutes or overtime), both better than the league norm by a wide margin
2 misses, 2 winsOpposing kickers missed potential game-tying or game-winning field goals against Brady's teams in the 2006 AFC Divisional Round (Nate Kaeding, 54 yards, wide) and the 2011 AFC Championship (Billy Cundiff), both misses directly kept New England's season alive
76.8%field goal percentage opponents made against New England from 2001–2019, the worst opposing-kicker accuracy allowed by any team in the league over that span. Meanwhile the Patriots' own kickers made 86.3% (+9.4 margin); no other team in the NFL was even at +4.0

The offensive line nobody talks about

Brady's blind side was protected by one of the most stable coaching setups in NFL history. Dante Scarnecchia ran New England's offensive line for effectively Brady's entire Patriots career, and the two seasons he wasn't there are the clearest natural experiment in the whole dynasty.

18 of 20Brady's Patriots seasons that Dante Scarnecchia spent as offensive line coach (1999–2013, 2016–2019), anchored for years by Logan Mankins, Dan Koppen, and Matt Light in front of him. The two seasons he wasn't there, 2014–2015, are the only real gap
21 sacksallowed during the 16–0 2007 regular season, 5th-fewest in the NFL, with Mankins, Light, and Koppen up front; Brady threw 50 touchdowns behind that line
The control groupScarnecchia retired after 2013; the very next season the line allowed 40 sacks, then kept struggling in 2014 under a new coach before he was coaxed out of retirement in 2016, after which New England won two more Super Bowls

For balance

The deck this dashboard is built from names two well-known moments that cut the other way: the 2001 "Tuck Rule" fumble briefly went against Brady before being overturned, and the 2007 "helmet catch" (David Tyree, Super Bowl XLII) is the signature unlucky moment of his career, costing New England a perfect season. Brady's teams also lost 11 playoff games. The argument here is about frequency and degree, not an unbroken run of good fortune.
